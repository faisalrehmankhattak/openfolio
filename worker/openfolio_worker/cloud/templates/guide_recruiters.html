Recruiters want to know three things fast: what you did, how well, and whether you fit the role. Put your most relevant work first, state your role and results plainly, and keep contact one click away. An AI twin goes one step further: recruiters can interview it at any time, by voice or chat, and it answers only from what you wrote.

{% endblock %} {% block body %}

What recruiters look for first

  1. Role fit. What you do and the level you work at, in one line.
  2. Evidence. Projects that show the problem, your part and the outcome.
  3. How you think. The decisions and trade-offs behind the work, not only the finished result.
  4. Communication. Whether you can explain your work clearly to someone outside your field.
  5. Practical details. Where you can work, when you are available and how to reach you.

Questions recruiters ask, and your portfolio should answer

  • "What exactly was your role on this project?"
  • "What result did it have?"
  • "Which tools and languages do you use day to day?"
  • "Why are you looking for a new role?"
  • "When could you start, and where can you work?"
  • "What's the best way to reach you?"

Write a one- or two-sentence answer to each. Those answers make your portfolio easier to scan, and they are the best material for an AI twin.

Structure your portfolio for a quick review

  • Open with one line: your role, your specialty and what you're looking for.
  • Show three to five projects, most relevant first.
  • For each project: the problem, your role, what you did and the result.
  • Keep contact details one click away on every page.
  • Make sure it loads fast and reads well on a phone.

How recruiters interview an AI twin

  1. Open the candidate's portfolio or Openfolio link.
  2. Tap Talk to speak or Chat to type. It is labelled AI from the start.
  3. Ask about projects, experience and skills. Answers come from what the candidate wrote and approved.
  4. If something isn't covered, it says so. Leave a message and the real candidate gets your name, email and note.

What an AI twin won't do

  • Make commitments for the candidate, such as accepting an offer, a salary or a start date.
  • Invent employers, dates or numbers it wasn't given.
  • Claim to be human.
  • Replace an interview. It is a faster first conversation, not a hiring decision.

Tips for candidates

  • Write your facts in your own words; your AI twin repeats them.
  • Add rules for topics you'd rather discuss in person, such as salary.
  • Test it with the questions above before you share it, and fix any answer that sounds wrong.
  • Put the link where recruiters already look: your résumé, LinkedIn profile and email signature.
